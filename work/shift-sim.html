Shift Sim: GLP-1 patient contacts

You are a non-clinical Patient Care Advocate. Six patients reach out about their GLP-1 weight management medicine. Your job is not to be the clinician. It is to know exactly what you can handle, what goes to a clinician, and what can't wait.

About 5 minutes. Two meters: Safety and Patient Trust. A safety miss always outweighs a warm answer.

Why it is built this way

  • No leaderboard. Ranking frontline staff on speed teaches the wrong thing in clinical work. Meters show the two things Ro cares about: safe care and an easy experience.
  • Safety outweighs warmth. A kind answer that misses a red flag scores worse than a blunt answer that escalates.
  • Wrong answers teach. Every option has its own feedback, because the wrong choices are the ones real people make.
  • Scope is the skill. Half the scenarios test whether the advocate stays out of clinical decisions. One tests whether they notice the problem is actually a shipping problem.

How it connects to performance

  • Each choice is tagged with a behavior from the QA rubric, such as "escalates red flags urgently."
  • Each choice sends a learning record (xAPI). You'll see one at the end.
  • Thirty days later, compare sim results with QA scores and escalation accuracy for the same behaviors. That is the test of whether the learning worked.
  • New scenarios come from real QA misses and are refreshed every quarter, with clinical sign-off.

Sample content for a portfolio. Clinical points are drawn from public FDA labeling for GLP-1 medicines and simplified for a non-clinical audience. This is not Ro's clinical protocol or escalation policy, and a real version would be written with and approved by Ro's clinical team. If you are a patient with a medical question, contact your clinician. In an emergency call 911. For a mental health crisis call or text 988.
