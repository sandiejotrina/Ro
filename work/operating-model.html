Start here: score the function

Adapted from the "Pack Your Wagon" self-check in my DevLearn session. 1 is never, 5 is always. Your lowest line tells you where to start. A tie goes to the earlier line, because the front door gets fixed first.

1. Intake: one front door

Every request, from a VP or a team lead, goes through the same short form and into one visible log. Nothing lives in someone's inbox.

The form

  1. Who is asking, and which team is affected?
  2. What did you see people doing, or not doing?
  3. What goes wrong for patients or the business if this doesn't change?
  4. How would we know it's fixed? Name a number if you have one.
  5. When is it needed, and what is driving that date?

Why question 3 matters

It turns "we need a course on X" into a problem statement. A request with no answer to question 3 goes back for clarification before anyone builds anything. It is the cheapest filter a learning team has.

Next step after intake: the triage. Training, job aid, knowledge fix, process or tool fix, or coaching.

3. Content governance: accurate, owned, retired on time

One source of truth

The knowledge base holds the facts. Courses, job aids, and scripts link to the article instead of copying it. When a protocol changes, one article changes, and everything pointing to it is current.

The review plan

  • One job per reviewer: clinical accuracy, compliance, and learner experience (two frontline staff).
  • All feedback in one place.
  • Two rounds, then it ships. Later feedback goes into the next version.
  • One named final approver. For clinical content, that is a clinician.

Every item has an owner and a review date. Anything with no owner or not opened in six months gets reviewed for retirement.

4. Measurement: four rungs

Completion rates show who sat through something, not whether it worked. Each rung has a data source and an owner, and the higher rungs belong to partners, not to L&D alone.

The feedback loop. QA misses feed new scenarios. Frontline "I couldn't find it" flags feed the knowledge base. Each launch ends with a four-question lessons-learned review, and one change goes into the playbook. The same behavior tags run through the sim, the QA rubric, and the dashboard, so the rungs can be joined.

5. Launch readiness

For a new medication, program, state, or tool. The goal is a frontline team that is ready on day one, with clinical sign-off on everything they will say.

6 weeks outScope the change with Product, Clinical, and Ops. Which roles, which contacts, which questions.
4 weeks outKnowledge articles drafted. Scenarios written from expected contacts. Clinical review starts.
2 weeks outPilot with a small group of frontline staff. Fix what confuses them.
Launch weekTraining done, job aid live, floor support and a fast lane for questions.
30 days afterRead QA and contact data. Lessons-learned review. Update content.

Readiness gate before launch: every article clinically approved, at least 95% of affected staff passed the scenario check, escalation paths tested end to end.

6. Team and roles

Training and knowledge management under one roof, because they fail together when they're split.

Learning design

Instructional designers who build onboarding, launch, and retraining programs from real contacts and QA data.

Knowledge management

Editors who own the knowledge base: structure, accuracy, review dates, and search findability.

Facilitation and onboarding

Trainers who run cohorts and floor support, often promoted from the frontline.

Learning ops and analytics

Runs intake, capacity, the LMS, and the measurement ladder. The CoE's operating system.

Clinical SME network

Named clinicians with protected time for review. They approve clinical content and own its accuracy.

Frontline champions

Experienced advocates who pilot content, flag gaps, and coach peers.

7. Technology and AI

Tool scorecard

Every LMS, authoring, knowledge, or analytics tool is scored before purchase:

  • Frontline experience: can an advocate find the answer mid-contact in under 30 seconds?
  • Maintainer experience: can one editor update a fact everywhere at once?
  • Data: does it send xAPI or clean exports that join to QA data?
  • Security and compliance: PHI handling, access controls, audit trail.
  • Total cost over three years, implementation time, and exit terms.

This is how I led a platform selection at Edwards: one-third lower three-year cost, 4 months to implement instead of 13, and exit rights tied to SLAs.

AI in the learning workflow

Where AI helps a learning team: first drafts of scenarios from de-identified QA patterns, first drafts of article updates when a protocol changes, tagging contacts by topic to spot gaps.

The guardrails, the same pattern I use for agentic workflows in a regulated function at Edwards:

  • Approved knowledge source only.
  • A human review gate. Clinical content gets clinician sign-off before anything is published.
  • No patient data in a tool that isn't approved for it.
  • Each output logged against the source it came from.
